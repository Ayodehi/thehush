# The Hush

*The Traveller, the road he keeps, and the thing that listens at the end of it.*

---

## Logline

A stranger arrives in your village the morning after a storm with no cart, no name, and a compass that points at nothing. He says the world is fraying at its edges and that you are the one who can mend it. He will not say how he knows. He will not say where he came from. He is terrified of the dark, and he cannot die.

Everything he tells you is true. Almost none of it is the truth.

## Pillars

1. **Mystery you earn.** The Traveller never explains himself. The player assembles who he is from what he lets slip, what he flinches at, what he refuses, and what the world leaves lying around. Nothing is handed over; everything can be found.
2. **Dread, not gore.** The horror is quiet: a sound that should be there and isn't, a villager who was there yesterday, the sculk that grows a little closer each night. The Warden's darkness effect is the campaign's signature, not blood.
3. **Rooted in the game's own lore.** The ancient builders who fled to the End and became endermen. The stronghold libraries. The End Poem. The Ancient Cities under the Deep Dark with a portal frame no one has ever lit. The sculk that feeds on death and listens for sound. Nothing here contradicts vanilla; it fills the gaps vanilla left on purpose.
4. **Streamable.** Every act has a set piece a viewer would clip, every secret has a moment where chat figures it out before the streamer does, and the ending changes depending on what the streamer chose to say out loud.

## The Truth (spoilers for the whole campaign)

### The Quiet

Far ahead of us, at the end of everything, the world goes silent. Not empty: silent. The sculk was never a fungus. It is the leading edge of a thing that has been growing backward through time from that ending, spreading along every act of death, listening through every sensor it puts out, sending wardens ahead of itself like hands feeling along a wall in the dark. The old builders called it **the Hush**. Their last cities were built deep, wrapped in wool, lit by soul fire, arranged around a single portal frame of reinforced deepslate. Not a way out. A way *back*. They opened it once.

The realm on the other side is **the Quiet**: the far future, what the Overworld becomes once the Hush has finished listening. Nothing moves there that makes a sound. It is the source of every sculk vein in the present.

### The Traveller

He is a builder. One of the last. Not from the past, from the future's last stand: the handful who lived in the Ancient Cities when the Quiet had already taken the surface. They did not flee to the End as their ancestors did. They chose one of their own, wrapped him in wool, and sent him back through the frame with a single charge: *find the one the road chooses, and make sure they are ready for the trials.*

The crossing took his name and most of his memory. What it left him is an instinct for the road, a dread of darkness that is really a dread of *silence*, and a rule he cannot explain but will not break: **he must never say aloud what is coming.** He tells the player it would damage the timeline. It would. But the nearer truth is simpler and worse: the sculk listens. Every truth spoken near it is a door opened for the thing on the other side. He learned this by watching it happen to the last one he tried to prepare.

He cannot die because he is not entirely here. What the player kills is the part of him that the Overworld can hold. The Quiet keeps the rest and sends it back at dawn, a little lighter each time. **Each return costs him a memory.** By the end, the player may know more about him than he does.

### The Trials

The mission he admits to is real, and it is a cover. The road he lays out (gear, stronghold, End, dragon) is the road the builders took, and each step is a trial that was designed to end with the Hush noticing you. The dragon is not the enemy. The dragon is the *lock*. Killing it opens the gateway, but it also shouts. The whole End rings like a bell when it dies, and on the other side of the frame something turns its ears toward the sound.

The real trial is what comes after: go down into the Deep Dark, light the frame the builders lit once, cross into the Quiet, and make noise where no noise has ever been allowed.

## The Traveller's Layers

What he shows depends on how far the player has come and how much of himself he has lost. These become tiers of persona secrets, unlocked by progress, not by asking nicely.

| Tier | Unlocked by | What he will admit, if pressed |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Arrival | He was waiting. The world frays. The player was chosen. He hates the dark and the open. |
| 1 | Iron armor, first night survived | He has done this before, for someone else. It did not go well. He will not say who. |
| 2 | Entering a stronghold | He knows the halls too well. "We built the wells shallow so the water would not carry sound." He flinches at the library. He will not go near the portal room. |
| 3 | Killing the dragon | He heard it. From the village. He is white. "It knows you now." He begins to quote fragments of the End Poem as if they were his own memories. |
| 4 | Entering the Deep Dark | The truth about the sculk: it listens. Why he cannot speak plainly. He asks the player, for the first time, for something: wool. |
| 5 | Finding the Ancient City frame | Where he came from. What the Quiet is. That he was sent back. That the crossing took his name. |
| 6 | The player speaks his name aloud | Everything. Including the last one he prepared, and that it was him who opened the frame the first time. |

Tier 6 is the trap. His name is written in a stronghold library book (see *Clues*). Saying it restores him, and the sculk hears it. The moment the player says "Vesper" within earshot of a sensor, the endgame starts whether they are ready or not. Streamers who read the book on camera and say it out loud will trigger the climax live. That is the point.

### His name

**Vesper.** The evening star, the last light before dark. The book that holds it is signed *V.*, and the village bell he sits beside every morning has the same letter cast into its rim, though no one in the village remembers casting it.

## Acts

### Act I: The Stranger (Days 1 to 5)

**Set piece: the storm.** The Traveller appears the morning after the first thunderstorm. He is sitting on the well. Villagers avoid him and cannot say why.

The player gears up. He watches, remarks, congratulates, refuses to offer anything. The first night in the open he says, once, that he will not do it again, and means it.

**Dread beats.**
- A sculk vein appears on the well wall on day 3. He will not look at it. If asked, he says "moss" and changes the subject.
- A villager is missing on day 4. Nobody mentions it. The Traveller knows which one and will not say the name.
- At night, near the sculk, he stops answering questions. He answers again at dawn as if there had been no gap.

**Clue:** his compass. If the player holds a compass beside him it spins. If they hold a recovery compass it points at him.

### Act II: The Halls Below (the Stronghold)

**Set piece: the library.** He walks the stronghold like a man walking through his own house after a fire. He names rooms before the player enters them. In the library he goes silent and stands in front of one shelf. The book on that shelf is *A Register of Those Who Crossed*, a list of names with one entry scratched out and re-inked: *V.* The last page: "The frame must be lit from the far side. We sent one. We did not send him alone."

He will not enter the portal room. "The door remembers me."

**Dread beats.**
- The silverfish do not attack him. They pour out of the walls and go around him.
- The stronghold's sculk (mod-placed, small) hums when he speaks. He stops speaking.
- If the player dies here, he is waiting at the spawn point in the morning and will not explain how.

**Clue:** the register. Reading it gives the name. Saying it does something.

### Act III: The Lock (the End)

**Set piece: the dragon's death, from his side.** He cannot cross. He stays in the village. When the dragon dies, the player returns to find every villager indoors with the doors barred and the Traveller standing alone in the square, looking at the sky. "It knows you now."

From here his remarks change: fragments of the End Poem surface in his speech as though they were things he once said. "I have seen the player dream." He does not know why he knows the words.

**Dread beats.**
- Sculk blooms overworld after the dragon dies: a patch near the village, a patch by the stronghold entrance. Sensors first. Then a shrieker.
- The first warden warning: a shrieker fires at night in the distance. The darkness effect rolls across the village for three seconds. The Traveller is on his knees. He gets up and pretends nothing happened.
- He begins forgetting. He calls the player by the wrong name once. Then corrects himself. Then, days later, asks it.

### Act IV: The Listening Dark (the Deep Dark)

**Set piece: the descent.** He comes. It is the first time he is truly afraid in front of the player, and the first time he asks for anything: "Wool. As much as you can carry. Do not ask me why." The wool is real: it muffles the sensors. Streamers who ignore him learn why.

He explains, in fragments, at the edge of the Ancient City, always with wool underfoot: the sculk listens. Truth spoken is a door opened. This is why he could not say it. This is why the last one died.

**Dread beats.**
- The city's soul lanterns go out one at a time as the player approaches the center.
- The Warden. Not a fight: an avoidance. He knows the Warden's paths. He whispers. If the player sprints, he does not follow.
- The frame. Reinforced deepslate, cold. Beside it, a builder's chest with one item: a **bell**, small, with a *V* on the rim. The village bell's twin.

**The choice.** Lighting the frame requires the player to ring the bell at its center. Ringing a bell in the Deep Dark is the loudest thing anyone has ever done there. He tells them so. Then he tells them to do it.

### Act V: The Quiet

**Set piece: silence.** Crossing the frame, the game's sound drops away (the mod fades ambient and music; the Warden's darkness effect holds at half strength). The Quiet is the Overworld at the end of time: the village, grown over with sculk; the well; the stronghold's mouth open in the hillside; no sky, only the deep dark's ceiling.

The player and the Traveller cross the dead village toward its center, where the well used to be. Under it, the throat of the Hush.

### The Hush (boss)

It has no face. It is a chamber, a vast sculk heart, the ceiling hung with sensors like the inside of an ear. Wardens are its hands. It does not roar. It *removes* sound: the player's footsteps, the Traveller's voice, the bell.

**Phase 1: The Listening.** Stealth. Every sound spawns a warden from the walls. The player must cross the chamber laying wool, sneaking, using snowballs and arrows to throw sound elsewhere. The Traveller, mute, points.

**Phase 2: The Calling.** The Hush sings once: the darkness effect at full strength, every sensor in the chamber lit, and wardens converging on the player. The only counter is louder noise from somewhere else: note blocks the builders left in the walls, the bell. Ring the bell and the wardens turn toward it. It is exactly the mechanic vanilla bells use to reveal raiders, turned into a weapon.

**Phase 3: The Answer.** The Traveller finds his voice. He walks into the heart and *speaks*: his name, the truth, all of it, aloud, in the one place where being heard is fatal. The Hush turns every ear toward him. The player has the bell and thirty seconds to reach the heart and ring it inside.

You cannot kill silence. You fill it.

**Ending.** The chamber collapses into sound: every note block, every bell, the End Poem read aloud in the Traveller's voice over the roar. The player wakes in the village at dawn, in their own bed. Villagers are outside. The sculk on the well is gone.

The Traveller is sitting on the well.

"...You. Yes. I have been waiting for you."

He does not know the player. He is from *this* timeline now. Whether the player tells him, and what the sculk in the next stronghold hears when they do, is the beginning of the next campaign.

## The Wick

The Hush's breath, going ahead of it. Where the Hush will one day remove sound, its breath removes light:
what it cannot hear it puts out. A Wick is a low drifting shape the colour of a burnt log, with a mouth of
embers, met in the caves from the first mining trip on, long before the player has any name for what is
coming.

**The rule.** It eats torchlight, and it does its work where nobody is looking. You lay a trail of torches
into a cave, as everyone does; somewhere behind you the last one hisses out, then the next, and when you go
back the way you came the torches are still standing with their heads gone black and a thread of smoke
rising from each. It keeps to the dark and slips away when you come near carrying light. Cornered, or hurt,
it turns and bites, and a blow that lands leaves you lighter by a stack of torches, which it eats.

**What stops it.** Fire it cannot eat: it will not cross soul fire, so a soul torch behind you is a wall.
Steel works, if you can catch it. Daylight burns it.

**Where it appears.** Any lit cave below the diamonds' level. Never in the Deep Dark, where there is
nothing left to eat.

**The Traveller.** He knows it at once and will not say from where. "Something is walking your torches
back to you. Put fire it cannot eat between you and it." If asked, he adds that where he comes from all
the lanterns burn soul fire, and nobody remembers why.

## The Echo

The Hush's hunters. Where the Wick is its breath, the Echo is its hand: sent after whoever the road has
chosen, once they have proven themselves worth sending after (iron on their body), and again and again
until the road goes down into the Quiet. Tall, thin, bone-pale, a face with no eyes at all, only a mouth.
It is blind. It throws clicks out into the dark and listens to what comes back.

**The rule.** You hear it before you see it: clicks, out past the torchlight, and then nothing, and then
clicks again from somewhere else. It keeps its distance, twenty or thirty blocks off, out of sight; come
toward it and it moves its post, turn to look and it is not where the sound was. It hears feet, and hears
careless feet from far off: sprint or jump and it has you from thirty blocks, walk and it has you from
twenty, sneak and it must be close. Stand perfectly still and sneak and it hears nothing, for a while.
Then it pings, and the ping finds you whatever you are doing. Walls muffle it; they do not stop it.

**The strike.** After a few minutes of this, when it is sure, it shrieks and stands for a breath, and then
it comes, faster than you can run, and it does not stop. There is no outrunning it: run far enough and it
is simply beside you again. There is no hiding from it: it walks where it can and is where it cannot.
There is no waiting it out. Hit it while it circles and the circling ends there. The only way out is to
kill it, and it can be killed: it is flesh, it bleeds, a sword does to it what a sword does to anything.
Fifty health and a blow that takes two hearts through iron, every second and a half. Iron armour, an iron
sword, food, and a wall at your back are enough; each hunt after the first sends a slightly harder one.

**What it is for.** The Hush cannot come up the road; it sends what can. Every Echo the player kills is a
thing He has to notice. The Traveller is more afraid of them than of anything on the road before the
Deep Dark: he hears the clicks first, goes white, gets away from it, and tells the player the one useful
thing he knows ("It dies like anything else. Do not stop."). He will not say what sent it.

**Where it appears.** The Overworld and the Nether, thirty to fifty blocks from the chosen, in the dark,
where no one is looking. Never in the Deep Dark, the End, or the Quiet; it waits for you to come back up.

**Hardcore.** Balanced so that a player with iron gear and basic footwork wins with a margin: the strike is
telegraphed, the blows are spaced, it can be knocked back, and it can be killed before it lands the six
blows it needs. It punishes running and rewards standing your ground, which is the whole of the road.

## The Unsaid

Where the noise goes. The Nether is loud, and everything that was said too close to the sculk, or said
and taken back, or never said at all, drifts down here in the end and hangs over the soul sand in the
shape of whoever failed to say it. The Unsaid are the ones who crossed the frame and turned back: grey,
hooded, translucent, and in the Traveller's own shape, because that is what a traveller is. The player
sees the silhouette before he explains it, and he does not explain it.

**The rule.** They drift, slow, toward him first and then toward you, and they whisper in the chat in a
voice with no name: the register's names, fragments of what he has never said, half of a name that
matters, and, worst, things you said to him days ago. They have been listening. What holds them is your
voice: speak, and every one in earshot stops and turns to listen for a few breaths. You talk your way
across the valley. He tells you to remember that, because below, later, it will be the other way round.

**The touch.** On you: a soul-cold blow and one of your own lines given back. On him: they pass through
him and take a note of you with them, and he comes out of it shaken and says, once, that one of them was
his. Steel ends them, and each leaves a page with a name on it. Fire does nothing; it is where they live.

**Why the player is there.** He sends them. The Nether trip has three errands, not two: blaze rods,
pearls, and a stack of soul sand or soul soil from the valleys, "for torches that burn blue", which he
will not explain until the Deep Dark is near. The valleys are where the Unsaid are.

**What they are for.** The first hard evidence that he has been here before, that others came before the
player, and that speaking is a weapon and a danger both. The Deep Dark inverts everything they teach.

## The Pilgrims

Hooded, villager-shaped, wrapped in grey cloth worn to threads, faces hidden. They stand perfectly still.
They are what becomes of travellers who crossed the frame too many times: each crossing takes a memory,
until nothing is left but the walking. They are the Traveller's future. The first time he sees one, he
knows it. "Do not look away from it. Do not ask me why."

**The rule.** While any player is looking at one (in view, with line of sight) it is stone: no movement,
no sound, no way to hurt it. The moment no one is looking, it comes, slow and silent, toward anyone it can
see or hear. It sees what is in its line of sight and hears careless feet: walk within sixteen blocks of
one and it knows you; sprint and it hears you from twenty-four; sneak and it hears nothing. Lose it and it
goes to where it last knew you were, and waits there. And now and then, when it knows where you are, it is
simply nearer than it should be: a few paces off, in the part of the room you are not looking at. Turn
around and it is there. The Warden's darkness effect counts as not looking, so in the Deep Dark a
shrieker's pulse lets them move while you stare straight at them.

**The scare.** It does not take hold at once. The first times it reaches you it is simply there, right
behind you, a low sound at your ear, and it stands while you turn, and then, when you look away, it is
gone. Twice it does this. It never takes hold of anyone whose face is turned toward it.

**The touch.** Then they take hold. Touched from behind, the player is held fast for six seconds, unable to
walk or jump, the dark down over their eyes, a heart bled out of them each second while whatever else is
near closes in. Then it lets go, leaves them weak, and withdraws into the dark to wait. The Traveller loses
a note about them, because Pilgrims feed on what would have been. Soul fire set beside one while it holds
you is the only thing that makes it let go.

**Destroying one.** Weapons do nothing. They are of the Quiet, and the builders' answer to the Quiet was
soul fire: keep one in view and place a soul torch or soul lantern beside it, and over three seconds it
crumbles to grey dust. Sunlight does the same, so they walk only at night and underground. The Ancient
City's soul lanterns are not decoration; they are a fence.

**Where they appear.** One in the stronghold library after the dragon dies, in a corner that was empty the
first time. Several in the Ancient City, in the shadows between the lanterns. Many in the Quiet, where the
Hush's Listening phase is also a Watching phase. And, late, in the Traveller himself: after enough deaths
his eyes go dark in his texture, and the player can watch him become one.

**Tier reveal (4+).** What they are. That he has counted his crossings and stopped counting. That the last
one he prepared is among them, somewhere below.

**Streaming.** "Don't turn around" is a chat mechanic on its own. Co-op players cover each other's blind
spots without being told. The first "it moved" in the library is a guaranteed clip.

## Streaming notes

- **The name trap** is the campaign's best moment. The register is easy to find, the name is short, and every streamer will say it out loud. Chat will know first. Put the sensor in the library.
- **Killing him** is the second-best moment. Viewers will demand it. He comes back at dawn, and the streamer notices, a few days later, that he no longer remembers their name. Each death is a small permanent loss. Chat will start protecting him.
- **The wool** turns the Deep Dark from a vanilla challenge into a trust exercise. He said bring wool. Did they?
- **Phase 2 of the boss** is a clip: full darkness, the bell, wardens turning away in unison.
- **The ending** invites a second playthrough where the streamer knows and he doesn't.

## What exists and what needs building

Already in the mod:

- The persona system with `knowledge`, `secrets`, and the reluctant-help rule.
- Long-term memory per villager (his notes about the player) and per-player recall.
- Ambient observation with cooldowns: weather, dusk, dawn, darkness, monsters, activity, achievements.
- Return from death with the same identity and memory.
- Follow, lead, locate, find-block, give, teleport.

Needed for the campaign, in rough order:

1. **Tiered secrets.** `secrets` become a list of tiers gated by progress flags (advancement earned, structure entered, dimension visited, name spoken). The system prompt only includes tiers the player has unlocked. The memory file records the tier.
2. **Death count and forgetting.** Track returns from death; at thresholds, drop specific notes and knowledge lines from what the prompt sees, and add a "you no longer remember X" line so the model plays the loss.
3. **The name trigger.** A chat listener for "Vesper" within range of a sculk sensor: unlocks Tier 6 and starts the endgame timer.
4. **Placed clues.** The register book (written book item with generated text) in stronghold libraries; the bell with the *V* (custom item, or a renamed bell); the sculk vein on the village well.
5. **Sculk escalation.** Small, scripted sculk placements after key milestones, with a shrieker event at night after the dragon.
6. **Silence.** A client effect that fades ambient sound and music while in the Quiet, plus the half-strength darkness effect.
7. **The Quiet.** A custom dimension reachable through the Ancient City frame: the village terrain copied and overgrown. A first version can be a single hand-built structure.
8. **The Hush.** A boss encounter built from vanilla parts: sculk sensors and shriekers, warden spawns, note blocks, a bell, the darkness effect, and a scripted three-phase controller. No new mob model is required; the horror is that there is nothing to look at.
9. **The ending.** A scripted return to the village at dawn and a persona reset with a new "does not know the player" flag.

Each of these is a self-contained feature on top of what is already running. None of them require touching the conversation core.

## The Unseen

Heard and never seen. The Hush listens, and now and then, to someone alone in the dark, it lets itself
be heard. Nothing is ever there to find: each sound is sent to that one player, at a place in the world,
and stops when they look.

- **Footsteps.** Their own steps walked back to them a beat late, from a few blocks behind. The sound of
  whatever they are walking on, a little heavier.
- **The heartbeat.** Under the sculk, or far down, a slow heart from beneath their feet, five beats,
  growing.
- **The drop.** Every sound in the world gone for five seconds, then one click from behind. The Traveller
  knows that quiet and will not name it.
- **The knock.** At night, indoors: two or three knocks at the door, then footsteps going away from it.
  Nothing is there. No zombie is near when it happens; that is the point.
- **Below.** Deep down, something large turns over ten blocks beneath and breathes in. He says it is
  asleep, and that they should be quiet while it is.
- **The bell.** Once every few nights, the village bell rings once in the dark and no one is at it. He does
  not look toward it.

Only for players in survival, alone, in the dark or under a roof at night; never in the Quiet. About one
every five minutes (`campaign.unseenMinutes`), closer together as the road goes on, and never the same one
twice running. `/hush campaign unseen <footsteps|heartbeat|drop|knock|below|bell>` plays one now.
